# How each pain is solved

This page takes each business pain from [The Pains](./the-pains.mdx), says in plain words how the platform solves it, and shows **where you can see the solution**. Every answer ends in a gold table. You can query that table in Athena (database `hospedaplus_gold`), and Power BI reads it from there. The gold tables are rebuilt every night and are ready by about 22:35 Colombia time (03:35 UTC).

| # | Pain | Solution in one sentence | Where to see it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | 37 % of bookings are cancelled | Measure the cancellation rate by channel, lead time and refundable/non-refundable, and send those rates back to the operation | `mart_p1_cancelacion`, `mart_p1_historico_f1`, Neon `operation.cancellation_rate_by_lead` |
| P2 | Uncontrolled overbooking | Every booking from every channel goes through one function that locks the inventory and respects a calculated overbooking limit | Neon `operation.reservar()`, `mart_p2_sobreventa` |
| P3 | Prices adjusted by hand, a week late | Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and pickup are calculated every night, together with a suggested price for the next 90 nights | `mart_p3_revenue`, `mart_p3_precio_sugerido` |
| P4 | Commissions not deducted | The commission is deducted from every booking in silver, so gross, commission and net revenue are known per channel | `mart_p4_comisiones` |
| P5 | Negative reviews not analysed | Reviews are deduplicated across platforms, classified by sentiment and turned into one reputation score per hotel and day | `mart_p5_reputacion`, `fact_resenas` |
| – | No single view of bookings | All channels (own engine, OTA, agencies) book through Neon, and silver keeps one row per booking | `fact_reservas`, `fact_ocupacion_diaria` |

## P1 · High cancellation rate

**The problem.** 37 % of bookings are cancelled, and the cancellation policy is the same for every channel. Nobody knew *which* bookings cancel.

**The solution.**
1. Silver computes, for every cancelled booking, how many hours before check-in it was cancelled and the penalty charged (`silver/cancelaciones`).
2. Gold groups bookings by **channel × lead-time bucket × refundable × month** and gives the cancellation rate, the penalty collected and the revenue lost (`mart_p1_cancelacion`). The Kaggle history adds the view by **deposit type** (`mart_p1_historico_f1`).
3. Every night `gold_neon_writeback` writes the rate per hotel and lead bucket to Neon (`operation.cancellation_rate_by_lead`). The operation uses that rate for the overbooking limits (P2), so the business and the operation work from the same number.
4. Neon has a cancellation policy table per channel type and refundable flag (`operation.cancellation_policies`), with a penalty that depends on how close to check-in the guest cancels.

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Channel, lead-time bucket and refundable flag, with their totals
SELECT channel_type, rango_anticipacion, reembolsable,
       SUM(reservas) AS reservas, SUM(canceladas) AS canceladas,
       -- Cancellation rate = cancelled / bookings (* 1.0 avoids integer division)
       ROUND(SUM(canceladas) * 1.0 / SUM(reservas), 3) AS tasa_cancelacion,
       -- Revenue lost to cancellations
       SUM(ingreso_perdido_cop) AS ingreso_perdido_cop
-- From the P1 cancellation mart
FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p1_cancelacion
-- One row per combination
GROUP BY channel_type, rango_anticipacion, reembolsable
-- Riskiest combinations first
ORDER BY tasa_cancelacion DESC;
```

## P2 · Uncontrolled overbooking

**The problem.** Each channel sold rooms on its own, so hotels sold more rooms than they had and had to relocate guests.

**The solution.**
1. **One door for every booking.** The booking engine (F3), the OTA consumer (F2) and the agency loader (F4) all call the same Neon function, `operation.reservar()`. It locks the requested nights with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, so two channels can never sell the last room at the same time:

   ```sql
   SELECT inventory_date, available_rooms
     FROM inventory
    WHERE hotel_id = v_hotel.id AND room_type_id = v_type.id
      AND inventory_date >= p_check_in AND inventory_date < p_check_out
    ORDER BY inventory_date
      FOR UPDATE
   ```

   If a night has no room left, the booking is refused and kept in `reservation_rejections`, so the demand that could not be served is measured too.
2. **A calculated limit.** `available_rooms` includes an `overbooking_limit` per hotel, room type and night: at most 5 % of the rooms, less if the cancellation rate is low, and **0 on nights with a high-impact local event** (F6). `refresh_overbooking_limits()` recalculates it from the P1 rates. It is switched off for now (`NEON_REFRESH_OVERBOOKING=false`), because switching it on changes how many rooms the hotels may sell.
3. Gold measures the result per hotel and night: rooms sold above capacity, relocations, rejections and % relocated (`mart_p2_sobreventa`).

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Rooms, sold, overbooking limit, oversold, relocated and rejected per hotel and day
SELECT hotel_code, fecha, habitaciones, vendidas, limite_sobreventa,
       sobrevendidas, reubicadas, habitaciones_rechazadas, pct_reubicacion
-- From the P2 overbooking mart
FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p2_sobreventa
-- Only days with overbooking or relocations
WHERE sobrevendidas > 0 OR reubicadas > 0
-- Newest days first
ORDER BY fecha DESC;
```

## P3 · Late, manual pricing

**The problem.** Prices were adjusted by hand once a week, without knowing the booking pace for each night.

**The solution.**
1. Every night gold calculates, per hotel and night, **occupancy %**, **ADR** (revenue ÷ rooms sold), **RevPAR** (revenue ÷ rooms available) and **pickup**: the rooms already booked 14 days before the night, compared with the same weekday a year earlier (`mart_p3_revenue`). Holidays (F6 Nager) and local events (F6) come as flags.
2. For the next 90 nights it suggests a price (`mart_p3_precio_sugerido`). The formula is the base rate × the pickup factor (1.10 when pickup is more than 15 % above last year, 0.92 when it is more than 15 % below) × 1.15 on holidays and event nights. The result always stays between 0.75 and 1.60 of the base rate (rules RN-05 and RN-06), and it is also shown in COP.
3. It is ready every night before 06:00 local time in every hotel, instead of a week later.

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Current rate, pickup and event factors, and the suggested price
SELECT hotel_code, room_type, fecha, tarifa, factor_pickup, factor_evento,
       precio_sugerido, precio_sugerido_cop
-- From the P3 suggested price mart
FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p3_precio_sugerido
-- Only the next 14 days
WHERE fecha BETWEEN current_date AND current_date + INTERVAL '14' DAY
-- By hotel and date
ORDER BY hotel_code, fecha;
```

## P4 · Commissions not deducted

**The problem.** Revenue was measured gross, so a channel with a high commission looked better than it was.

**The solution.** Silver converts every booking to COP with the exchange rate of the booking day (F7) and deducts the channel's commission (`comision_cop`, `neto_cop`). Gold adds it up by **channel × hotel × month**, with the effective commission % (`mart_p4_comisiones`). Cancelled stays are left out.

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Gross, commission and net per channel type
SELECT channel_type, SUM(bruto_cop) AS bruto_cop, SUM(comision_cop) AS comision_cop,
       SUM(neto_cop) AS neto_cop,
       -- Commission as a % of the gross
       ROUND(SUM(comision_cop) * 100.0 / SUM(bruto_cop), 2) AS pct_comision
-- From the P4 commissions mart
FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p4_comisiones
-- One row per channel type
GROUP BY channel_type
-- The channel that leaves the most net money first
ORDER BY neto_cop DESC;
```

## P5 · Negative reviews not analysed

**The problem.** Reviews arrived from several platforms, often the same review twice, and nobody tracked them.

**The solution.** Silver keeps the newest version of each review, removes the copies of the same review published on another platform, and gives each review a sentiment band from its rating (`silver/resenas`, details in [Silver layer](./data-engineering-pipeline/medallion/silver.mdx)). Gold computes for every hotel and day the **RN-10 score**: the average of the last 90 days, with the last 30 days weighing double, plus the % of positive and negative reviews (`mart_p5_reputacion`).

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Rating out of 10, reviews of the last 90 days and % positive/negative
SELECT hotel_code, fecha_corte, calificacion_rn10, resenas_90d, pct_positivas, pct_negativas
-- From the P5 reputation mart
FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p5_reputacion
-- Only the newest cut-off date
WHERE fecha_corte = (SELECT MAX(fecha_corte) FROM hospedaplus_gold.mart_p5_reputacion)
-- Hotels with the most negative reviews first
ORDER BY pct_negativas DESC;
```

## No single view of bookings

**The problem.** Bookings arrived from a channel manager, agency files and the own engine, each in its own place.

**The solution.** Every channel books through Neon (`reservar()`), so there is one list of bookings and one inventory. The lakehouse copies it every hour, and silver keeps **one row per booking** with the same columns for every channel (`silver/reservas`). In gold, `fact_reservas` and `fact_ocupacion_diaria` are that single view. Duplicate agency bookings (F4) are marked and never counted.

**Where to see it.**

```sql
-- Bookings and revenue per source (F1-F4) and channel type
SELECT source, channel_type, COUNT(*) AS reservas, SUM(total_cop) AS total_cop
-- From the bookings fact
FROM hospedaplus_gold.fact_reservas
-- One row per source and channel type
GROUP BY source, channel_type;
```

## Where in the pipeline each pain is solved

The tables below say, for each pain, **in which phase** the solution happens, **which DAG** runs it and **which file and function** contain the code. All paths are in the [hospedaplus-pipeline](https://github.com/HospedaPlus/hospedaplus-pipeline) repo, except the Neon functions, which live in [db_simulated](https://github.com/HospedaPlus/db_simulated).

Some pains are solved **at the source** (in Neon, before the data reaches the lake) and others **in the pipeline** (silver or gold). P1 is solved in both: the pipeline calculates the rate and then writes it back to the source.

### P1 · Cancellations

| Phase | DAG | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | `bronze_neon_f2_f3` (hourly) and `bronze_neon_full_load` (03:00 UTC) | `dags/bronze_neon_f2_f3.py`, `dags/bronze_neon_full_load.py` | Copies the bookings, with their status and cancellation date, from Neon to `bronze/` |
| Silver | `silver_reservas_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_reservas.py` | Calculates the hours before check-in for each cancellation and its lead-time bucket → `silver/cancelaciones` |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p1_cancelacion()` | Rate by channel × lead bucket × refundable × month, with lost revenue |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `neon_cancellation_rates()` | Rate per hotel and lead bucket over the stays of the last year → `gold/neon_cancellation_rates.json` |
| Write-back | `gold_neon_writeback` | `dags/gold_neon_writeback.py` · `upsert()` | `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (hotel_code, lead_bucket) DO UPDATE` into `operation.cancellation_rate_by_lead` |

```python
# spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py
# Mart P1: cancellations per channel, lead time, refundable and month
def mart_p1_cancelacion(fr):
    # Month of the check-in, e.g. 2026-11
    return (fr.withColumn("anio_mes", F.date_format("check_in", "yyyy-MM"))
            # One group per combination
            .groupBy("channel_code", "channel_type", "rango_anticipacion", "reembolsable", "anio_mes")
            # Bookings and cancelled bookings of each group
            .agg(F.count("*").alias("reservas"),
                 F.sum(F.col("es_cancelada").cast("int")).alias("canceladas"), ...)
            # Rate = cancelled / bookings
            .withColumn("tasa_cancelacion", F.round(F.col("canceladas") / F.col("reservas"), 4)))
```

**Example.** An OTA booking for 15 Nov is cancelled on 13 Nov at 10:00. Silver calculates 53 hours before check-in (15:00) and puts it in the `"1-3 dias"` bucket. That night gold counts it in its channel's row of `mart_p1_cancelacion`. `gold_neon_writeback` then updates that hotel's rate in Neon, and the operation sees it the next morning.

### P2 · Overbooking

| Phase | Where | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source (OLTP) | Neon, called by F2, F3 and F4 | `db_simulated` · `operation.reservar()` | Locks the nights with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`; refuses the booking if a night has no room left and records it in `reservation_rejections` |
| Source (OLTP) | Neon | `db_simulated` · `operation.refresh_overbooking_limits()` | Recalculates `overbooking_limit` from the P1 rates; 0 on high-impact event nights |
| Write-back | `gold_neon_writeback` | `dags/gold_neon_writeback.py` | Calls `refresh_overbooking_limits()` only if `NEON_REFRESH_OVERBOOKING=true` (off today) |
| Silver | `silver_ocupacion_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_ocupacion.py` | Inventory, relocations and rejections per hotel, room type and night |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p2_sobreventa()` | Rooms sold above capacity, relocations, rejections and % relocated |

**Example.** Two channels try to sell the last Deluxe room for the same night at the same second. The first call to `reservar()` locks that inventory row; the second waits, then finds `available_rooms = 0`, is refused and lands in `reservation_rejections`. The next night `mart_p2_sobreventa` shows one rejected room and zero rooms sold above capacity.

### P3 · Pricing

| Phase | DAG | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | `bronze_f6_holidays`, `bronze_f6_events`, `bronze_f7_fx`, `bronze_neon_dims` | `dags/bronze_f6_*.py`, `dags/bronze_f7_fx.py`, `dags/bronze_neon_dims.py` | Holidays, local events, exchange rates and base rates |
| Silver | `silver_calendario_daily`, `silver_fx_daily`, `silver_ocupacion_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_calendario.py`, `silver_fx.py`, `silver_ocupacion.py` | Calendar per hotel with holiday/event flags, one rate per currency and day, occupancy per night |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p3_revenue()`, `on_the_books()` | Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and pickup vs. the same weekday a year earlier |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p3_precio_sugerido()` | Suggested price for the next 90 nights; `check()` fails the run if a price leaves the RN-06 band |

```python
# spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py · mart_p3_precio_sugerido()
# Pickup above the threshold -> raise 10 % (1.10); below -> lower 8 % (0.92); otherwise 1.0
.withColumn("factor_pickup", F.when(F.col("pickup_pct") > PICKUP_THRESHOLD_PCT, PICKUP_ABOVE)
            .when(F.col("pickup_pct") < -PICKUP_THRESHOLD_PCT, PICKUP_BELOW).otherwise(1.0))
# Price = rate x pickup factor x event factor, never below 75 % (0.75) or above 160 % (1.60) of the base rate
.withColumn("precio_sugerido", F.round(F.least(F.col("tarifa_base") * PRICE_CAP,
            F.greatest(F.col("tarifa_base") * PRICE_FLOOR,
                       F.col("tarifa") * F.col("factor_pickup") * F.col("factor_evento"))), 2))
```

**Example.** A Deluxe room has a base rate of 560,000 COP. For a night with a local event, 14 days before it 20 % more rooms are booked than last year. The suggestion is 560,000 × 1.10 × 1.15 = 708,400 COP, which is inside the band (420,000 to 896,000).

### P4 · Commissions

| Phase | DAG | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | `bronze_neon_dims`, `bronze_f7_fx` | `dags/bronze_neon_dims.py`, `dags/bronze_f7_fx.py` | Channels with their `commission_pct`, and the exchange rates |
| Silver | `silver_reservas_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_reservas.py` | Converts to COP and deducts the commission: `comision_cop`, `neto_cop` |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p4_comisiones()` | Gross, commission and net per channel × hotel × month, with the effective % |

```python
# spark_jobs/silver_reservas.py
# Commission in COP = total x commission % / 100
.withColumn("comision_cop", F.round(F.col("total_cop") * F.col("comision_pct") / 100, 2))
# Net = total - commission
.withColumn("neto_cop", F.col("total_cop") - F.col("comision_cop"))
```

**Example.** A 1,680,000 COP stay sold through an OTA with an 18 % commission leaves 302,400 COP of commission and 1,377,600 COP net. The same stay on the own website (0 %) leaves the full 1,680,000 COP, and `mart_p4_comisiones` shows that difference per channel.

### P5 · Reviews

| Phase | DAG | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | `bronze_f5_reviews` (hourly) | `dags/bronze_f5_reviews.py` | Reads the reviews API incrementally with `updated_from` |
| Silver | `silver_resenas_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_resenas.py` | Keeps the newest version of each review, removes the same review published on another platform, adds the sentiment band |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `mart_p5_reputacion()` | RN-10 score: 90 days, the last 30 weighing double; % positive and negative |

```python
# spark_jobs/silver_resenas.py
# Same hotel, stay date, score and text = the same review on another platform
key = ["hotel_code", "fecha_estadia", "puntaje", "_texto_norm"]
# first one wins
w_dup = Window.partitionBy(*key).orderBy("publicada_en", "review_id")
```

**Example.** A guest posts the same 2-star text on two platforms. Silver keeps the first one, records in `duplicados` that there were 2 copies, and marks it `negativo`. In gold it counts once in the hotel's `pct_negativas`.

### Single view of bookings

| Phase | Where | File · function | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source (OLTP) | Neon | `db_simulated` · `operation.reservar()` | F2, F3 and F4 all book in the same tables; a repeated agency booking gets status `duplicate` |
| Silver | `silver_reservas_daily` | `spark_jobs/silver_reservas.py` | The newest version of each booking, the same columns for every channel, `es_duplicado` |
| Gold | `gold_modelo_daily` | `spark_jobs/gold_modelo.py` · `fact_reservas()` | `filter("NOT es_duplicado")`: duplicates are never counted |

## Example: one booking from start to finish

This is how one booking moves through every phase, and where it helps each pain.

1. **Source (14:02 UTC).** An OTA sends a booking through Kafka. The `neon-consumer` calls `operation.reservar()`, which locks the nights and writes the booking (P2).
2. **Bronze (15:00 UTC).** `bronze_neon_f2_f3` copies the bookings of the 14:00–15:00 interval to `bronze/f3_reservations/fecha=2026-11-10/hora=14.jsonl`.
3. **Silver (after bronze).** `silver_reservas_daily` starts by itself through its Asset. It converts the amount to COP, deducts the commission (P4) and keeps one row per booking (single view).
4. **Gold (~03:35 UTC).** `gold_modelo_daily` adds the booking to `fact_reservas`, to the night's occupancy and pickup (P3), and to the cancellation rates if it is cancelled later (P1).
5. **Write-back.** `gold_neon_writeback` updates `operation.cancellation_rate_by_lead` in Neon (P1).
6. **Analytics.** Power BI reads gold through Athena, and the Revenue Manager sees the result before 06:00.

## How to open these results

1. **Athena**: AWS console, region us-east-2 → Athena → *Query editor* → database `hospedaplus_gold`, then run any of the queries above. Setup in [Athena configuration](./data-engineering-pipeline/athena.mdx).
2. **Power BI**: connect with the Amazon Athena connector to the `hospedaplus_gold` database. Each pain has its own mart, so each report page reads one small table.
3. **Neon**: the rates written back for P1 are in `operation.cancellation_rate_by_lead`, and the P2 refusals are in `operation.reservation_rejections`.
